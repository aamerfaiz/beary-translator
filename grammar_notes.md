# Beary (Byari) — Phonology & Grammar Notes

Everything below is taken from the front matter of the **Beary–Kannada–English
Lexicon** (*Nigantu*), Karnataka Beary Sahitya Academy, 2017 — the phonology
note by Prof. B.A. Viveka Rai and the Editorial by B.M. Ichlangod,
B.A. Shamshuddin Madikeri and Abdul Rahman Kuthethoor. Page references are to
the Roman-numeral front matter (`docs/PronunciationandStuff.PDF`).

This file supersedes an earlier version of these notes that was assembled from
web sources and inference. Where the two disagreed, the Academy's text wins.

## 1. What Beary is

- A **Dravidian language** spoken by the Beary community of coastal Karnataka
  (Dakshina Kannada, Udupi) and Kasaragod in Kerala, by roughly 1.5 million
  people. The Academy's editors describe it as an independent Dravidian
  language and "one of the sister languages of Kannada", alongside Tulu,
  Konkani and Kodava.
- It has borrowed from **Arabic** (religious and household vocabulary),
  **Tamil, Tulu, Malayalam, Kannada** and modern **English**. The editors are
  explicit that borrowed words are treated as Beary words, not foreign ones —
  *Computer*, *College* (English), *Bojja*, *Nema* (Tulu), *Upakara*,
  *Prayoga* (Kannada) all appear as headwords.
- Historically a **spoken** language with no script of its own. Bearys write it
  in Kannada script; the Lexicon adds a Roman transliteration column so that
  speakers outside Karnataka, and non-Kannada readers, can use it. **This
  project uses only that Roman transliteration.**

> Note: an earlier version of these notes called Beary "a Malayalam dialect
> with Tulu phonology". The Academy does not describe it that way. Many Beary
> words are pronounced like Malayalam words because of the community's
> religious ties to Kerala, but that is contact, not descent.

## 2. Sound inventory (pp. xxxix–xlii)

**Vowels — 10, plus 2 special.**

|        | Front | Central | Back |
|---|---|---|---|
| High | i ī | ụ | u ū |
| Mid  | e ē |     | o ō |
| Low  | ɛ   | a ā |      |

Rounded: `u ū ụ o ō`. Unrounded: `i ī e ē a ā`.
The two special vowels are **ụ** (central, high) and **ɛ** (low front).

**Consonants — 23, including 6 nasals.**

|            | Voiceless | Voiced | Nasal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Velar      | k  | g  | ṅ |
| Palatal    | c  | j  | ñ |
| Retroflex  | ṭ  | ḍ  | ṇ |
| Dental     | t  | d  | n, n' |
| Labial     | p  | b  | m |

Unclassified: `y r l v ś s h`.
Created for Arabic loanwords: `kh`, `jh`, `ph` — pronounced unlike `k`, `j`, `p`.

**There are no aspirated sounds in native Beary words.**

`n'` is a distinct alveolar nasal, separate from `n`, and it is contrastive:
*n'ōkụ* (see), *n'āvu* (tongue).

## 3. Pronunciation key (p. xli–xlii)

| | nearest English | | nearest English |
|---|---|---|---|
| a | *u* of "cut"    | ṭ  | *t* of "neat" (retroflex) |
| ā | *a* of "calm"   | ḍ  | *d* of "day" (retroflex) |
| i | *i* of "sit"    | ṇ  | no English equivalent (retroflex) |
| ī | *ee* of "keep"  | n' | *n* of "synthetic", "anthem" |
| u | *u* of "put"    | t  | *th* of "thief" |
| ū | *oo* of "shoot" | d  | *th* of "they" |
| ɛ | *e* of "men"    | ś  | *sh* of "shame" |
| e | *e* of "bet"    | kh | no English equivalent; Arabic *kh* in "Khalifa" |
| ē | *a* of "mate"   | jh | *z* of "zero" |
| o | *o* of "obey"   | ph | *f* of "father" |
| ō | *o* of "note"   | ṅ  | *ng* of "sing" |
| c | *ch* of "chicken" | ñ | *n* of "bench" |

`ṭ ḍ ṇ` are retroflex — the tongue tip curls back. English has no retroflex
consonants, so those equivalents are approximate.

**Anusvara.** Written `o` in Kannada script; in transliteration it becomes the
nasal matching the following consonant: `ṅ` before k/g (and kh), `ñ` before c/j
(and jh), `ṇ` before ṭ/ḍ, `n`/`n'` before t/d, and `m` before p, b, y, r, l, v,
ś, s, h (and ph).

## 4. Word formation (p. xlvii)

**Feminine — `-ti`:**
sorakāra (male worker) → sorakārti · pirānda (mad man) → pirānti ·
yajamāna (male owner) → yajamānti

**Plural — `-ṅ`, `-mār`, `-rụ`:**
dāda (elder sister) → dādaḍaṅa · caṅṅāyi (friend) → caṅṅāyimārụ ·
bālakkāra (young person) → bālakkārụ · pōṇḍa (he goes) → pōṇḍārụ (they go)

**Causative — `-bāṭụ` / `-pāṭụ`** (interchangeable):
ākụ (to do) → ākụbāṭụ / ākụpāṭụ (to make someone do) ·
ōḍu (to run) → ōḍụpāṭụ / ōḍụbāṭụ

**Interchangeable endings `-ro`/`-ḍo` and `-re`/`-ḍe`:**
cellụḍo = cellụro (saying) · ākụḍo = ākụro (doing) ·
pandurɛ = panduḍɛ (flicker) · piḍikurɛ = piḍikuḍɛ (I hold / I catch)

## 5. Sentence structure

Beary follows the general Dravidian pattern: **Subject–Object–Verb**,
postpositions rather than prepositions, and no definite or indefinite articles.

The Academy notes that no grammar of Beary has yet been written, and that the
Lexicon marks only **nouns and verbs**; pronouns, adjectives, adverbs and
interjections are deliberately left untagged. So the dictionary data in this
repo has a part of speech for roughly 86% of entries and blank for the rest —
that blank means "not marked in the source", not "unknown word".

## 6. Things the editors flag as limitations

- Beary has **considerable regional variation** (Mangalore, Udupi/Kundapura to
  the north, Kumble/Manjeshwara/Kasargod/Vittla/Puttur/Sullia to the south).
  The Lexicon deliberately includes variants rather than picking one, which is
  why many entries carry `see_also` forms with the same gloss.
- Many words carry **more than one meaning**, more so than in neighbouring
  languages, and the editors kept all of them.
- Some vocabulary has been **lost** with the decline of traditional crafts and
  household objects. The editors note there is no Beary word for "heart".
- The Lexicon documents **about 20,000 words** and the editors describe it as a
  first pass, expecting corrections in later editions.
